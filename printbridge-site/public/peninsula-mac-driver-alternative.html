A Peninsula Mac driver alternative for Zebra printers

Two ways to drive a Zebra label printer from a Mac, side by side.

Because Zebra doesn’t publish an official macOS driver for its label printers, Mac users who want good labels look for a third-party driver. Peninsula sells one for thermal printers; CrispDock is another, made for Zebra ZPL printers. Both install as macOS printer drivers. Here’s how they compare on the points you can check publicly.

When Peninsula may suit you

If your Mac runs a macOS version older than 13, Peninsula’s driver supports macOS 10.14 and later, while CrispDock needs macOS 13. And if you prefer a single payment that includes a year of upgrades, that is how Peninsula’s licence works.

When CrispDock may suit you

  • Lower cost: Pro is $29.99 a year or $79 once, and the free version never expires.
  • Try it fully first: every feature works in the free version, and Pro starts with 7 days free.
  • iPhone and iPad: AirShare shares your Mac’s Zebra so phones and tablets can print to it with AirPrint.
  • Made for Zebra ZPL: sharp barcodes, continuous printing, the same 4×6 size from any app, and out-of-paper or cover-open status reported.

Trying CrispDock

Download the free version and run the installer with your Zebra connected; it switches the Zebra printers it finds to the CrispDock driver and keeps their names. Uninstalling CrispDock restores the built-in macOS driver, so if you go back to another driver you’d reinstall it.

Questions

How much cheaper is CrispDock than Peninsula?

Peninsula is £97 (about $118 at checkout) per computer as a one-time purchase with a year of upgrades. CrispDock Pro is $29.99 a year or $79 lifetime, and the free version costs nothing.

Do both cut labels out of letter-size sheets?

Yes. Both can take a shipping label printed on a letter-size page and print it as a 4×6 label.

Can I try CrispDock before buying?

Yes. The free version has every feature with a watermark, and Pro’s yearly plan starts with a 7-day free trial (card required, once per Mac).

Does CrispDock run on older macOS versions?

No. CrispDock needs macOS 13 or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.

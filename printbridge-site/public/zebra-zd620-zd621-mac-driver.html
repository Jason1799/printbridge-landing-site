Zebra ZD620 / ZD621 driver for Mac

The ZD620 and its successor the ZD621 sit at the top of Zebra’s 4-inch desktop range. They print faster than the ZD42x models — up to 8 inches per second at 203 dpi — which makes them a common choice for busy shipping and warehouse stations.

Speed is wasted if the printer keeps stopping to wait for data, and that is what the generic macOS driver tends to cause. Zebra itself offers no official macOS driver for its label printers (support article 000022631). CrispDock is a macOS driver that sends each label as compact ZPL in one piece, so the printer can run at its pace.

Why the built-in Mac driver makes ZD620 or ZD621 labels blurry

Without a Zebra driver, macOS uses its generic “Zebra ZPL Label Printer” driver, which sends each page as a picture. A thermal head can only print a dot or leave it blank, so soft edges and greys become a speckled dot pattern: fuzzy barcodes. It also tends to stop and start mid-label and get sizes wrong. CrispDock converts each page into clean ZPL instead, so bars land on the printer’s dot grid and each label goes over in one piece. Why labels blur, in detail.

Labels on letter sheets

Many sites hand you a shipping label on a letter-size page, which the built-in driver shrinks onto the 4×6 label. With CrispDock the ZD620 or ZD621 gets just the label: found on the letter or A4 sheet, cut out and rotated to fill 4×6, with no cropping at the packing bench. That covers USPS, UPS, FedEx (including international), DHL, TikTok Shop, Amazon return “cut here” pages and letter-size labels from marketplaces and carrier sites. Platform-by-platform guide.

Built for volume

When you print a batch, CrispDock sends each label in one piece so the ZD620 or ZD621 runs smoothly from one label to the next instead of pausing partway through. The 300 dpi versions are supported too, at their own top speed of 6 in/s.
